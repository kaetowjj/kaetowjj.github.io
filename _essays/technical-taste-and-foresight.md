Technical knowledge and technical judgment are not the same thing. Knowing how a technology works differs from understanding why it emerged, whether it deserves investment, and what it might make possible.

In 2006, NVIDIA introduced CUDA. Deep learning was not mainstream and large language models were far from today's reality. Turning a graphics processor into a general-purpose computing platform was not an obvious commercial choice. What evidence supported that decision, and why was it worth sustained investment before the market matured?

Technical papers explain how systems are designed; biographies, technological history and the history of scientific ideas help explain the choices behind them. This is a reading map, not a checklist to complete in order.

## 0. Learning to read

### How to Read a Book

Mortimer J. Adler & Charles Van Doren · 1940 / 1972

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780671212094-M.jpg?default=false" alt="How to Read a Book cover" width="120" loading="lazy" />

Different books require different approaches. Adler and Van Doren distinguish elementary, inspectional, analytical and syntopical reading. The last is particularly useful here: compare Huang, Grove and Licklider around a shared question rather than treating each biography as an isolated success story.

## 1. Research taste and independent thinking

### The Art of Doing Science and Engineering

Richard W. Hamming · 1997

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9781732265172-M.jpg?default=false" alt="The Art of Doing Science and Engineering cover" width="120" loading="lazy" />

Drawing on his experience at Bell Labs, Hamming discusses learning, creativity and the choice of research problems. The point is not to become another Hamming, but to learn to recognize important problems and avoid spending years on work of limited value.

### Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!

Richard P. Feynman · 1985

Feynman's explorations of lock-picking, painting and biology illustrate curiosity beyond professional boundaries. Independent thinking can grow from play and experiment as well as formal training.

### A Mind at Play

Jimmy Soni & Rob Goodman · 2017

Claude Shannon connected mathematical logic, circuits and communication, laying foundations for information theory. His story invites us to examine how abstractions reveal common structures across apparently unrelated fields.

### The Man from the Future

Ananyo Bhattacharya · 2021

Von Neumann's work spans mathematics, computing, physics and game theory. His biography shows how deep foundations support interdisciplinary thinking and how abstractions migrate between fields.

## 2. Technological imagination and foresight

### The Dream Machine

M. Mitchell Waldrop · 2001

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9781732265110-M.jpg?default=false" alt="The Dream Machine cover" width="120" loading="lazy" />

Centered on J. C. R. Licklider, this history follows interactive computing, personal computing and computer networks. In an era dominated by batch processing, Licklider imagined computers as tools for thought; research funding, communities and experiments helped make that vision real.

### The Nvidia Way

Tae Kim · 2024

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9781324086719-M.jpg?default=false" alt="The Nvidia Way cover" width="120" loading="lazy" />

NVIDIA's history is also the evolution of a computing platform. CUDA opened GPU parallelism to general-purpose programmers, supporting scientific computing, engineering and eventually deep learning. Examine how an established business funded long-term investment and a developer ecosystem under uncertainty.

### Steve Jobs

Walter Isaacson · 2011

Jobs offers a different kind of technical judgment: making choices across engineering, design, the humanities and user experience. A technically feasible product is not necessarily a good one; deciding what to omit matters.

### The Innovators

Walter Isaacson · 2014

From early computing ideas to transistors, personal computers and the internet, this history connects generations of contributors. Technological revolutions often emerge when accumulated knowledge, engineering capabilities and social needs converge.

## 3. Industrial change and strategic judgment

### Only the Paranoid Survive

Andrew S. Grove · 1996

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780385483827-M.jpg?default=false" alt="Only the Paranoid Survive cover" width="120" loading="lazy" />

Grove examines strategic inflection points. Intel's shift from memory chips to microprocessors illustrates how a company can recognize change, abandon a previously successful path and reallocate resources.

### The Innovator's Dilemma

Clayton M. Christensen · 1997

Why can well-managed companies that listen to customers still miss emerging technologies? Christensen offers a framework for understanding disruptive innovation. Test its explanatory power against actual cases rather than labeling every technological change disruptive.

### Chip War

Chris Miller · 2022

Semiconductors depend on materials, manufacturing, equipment, design software, capital and international specialization. This history places NVIDIA, Intel, TSMC and ASML within a broader industrial ecosystem.

### Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital

Carlota Perez · 2002

Perez studies technological revolutions, finance and economic development. Her framework helps examine investment booms, infrastructure built ahead of mature applications, and the relationship between financial bubbles and lasting industrial value.

## 4. How innovation actually happens

### The Idea Factory

Jon Gertner · 2012

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780143122791-M.jpg?default=false" alt="The Idea Factory cover" width="120" loading="lazy" />

Bell Labs illustrates how sustained investment, concentrated talent and interdisciplinary exchange produced advances including the transistor and information theory. Consider the institutional conditions that enable research without obvious near-term commercial returns.

### The Soul of a New Machine

Tracy Kidder · 1981

This account of engineers building a new computer focuses on the concrete realities of engineering: deadlines, technical disagreements, teamwork and design decisions under imperfect conditions.

### Skunk Works

Ben R. Rich & Leo Janos · 1994

The story of Lockheed's advanced development team covers the U-2, SR-71 and F-117. It shows how small teams use autonomy, communication and rapid decisions to undertake complex, uncertain engineering projects.

### Where Good Ideas Come From

Steven Johnson · 2010

Johnson examines accumulated knowledge, the adjacent possible, serendipity and open exchange. Read alongside The Idea Factory and A Mind at Play to compare individual creativity with its environment.

## 5. A broader scientific and historical perspective

### The Structure of Scientific Revolutions

Thomas S. Kuhn · 1962

Kuhn examines how scientific knowledge develops within paradigms and how those paradigms change. His work offers a perspective on scientific communities, knowledge systems and major theoretical shifts.

### The Beginning of Infinity

David Deutsch · 2011

Deutsch discusses explanation, knowledge growth and scientific progress. Consider what makes an explanation good and how new knowledge can change seemingly insurmountable constraints.

### The Making of the Atomic Bomb

Richard Rhodes · 1986

From fundamental physics to the Manhattan Project, Rhodes traces how discoveries became world-changing technology through state resources, engineering and historical circumstances. Growing technical capability does not automatically imply social progress.

### The Wright Brothers

David McCullough · 2015

The brothers used experiments, measurement and iteration to solve key problems in powered flight. Their emphasis on flight control, rather than engine power alone, offers a lesson in problem selection.

## How to use this list

The aim is not to find a reproducible formula for success. Biographies often center on eventual achievements, but the most revealing moments may be those when the outcome was still unknown.

- What information was available, and which trends were visible or impossible to predict?
- What alternatives existed, and what would a wrong decision have cost?
- Why did contemporaries choose differently? What failed cases provide a counterexample?

Technical papers, biographies and history complement each other: papers explain internal structure, history explains the conditions of emergence, and lives reveal judgment and choice. The value of sustained reading is not predicting the next world-changing technology with certainty, but developing better-grounded judgment.

技术知识和技术判断力并不是一回事。掌握一项技术，意味着知道它如何工作、如何使用，以及如何解决实际问题。但判断一项技术是否值得投入，理解它为什么会在某个时代出现，或者识别它未来可能产生的影响，则需要另一种能力。

2006 年，NVIDIA 推出 CUDA。那时深度学习尚未成为主流，大语言模型也远未出现。将 GPU 从图形处理器发展为通用计算平台，并不是一个显而易见的商业选择。为什么有人能够提前看到这种计算方式的潜力？他们的判断建立在什么基础上？又是什么让他们愿意在市场尚未成熟时持续投入？

技术论文可以解释一个系统如何被设计出来，而人物传记、技术史和科学思想史可以帮助我们理解设计背后的选择。这不是一份必须按顺序完成的清单，而是一张可以长期使用的阅读地图。

## 0. 学习如何阅读

### How to Read a Book

Mortimer J. Adler & Charles Van Doren · 1940 / 1972

《如何阅读一本书》

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780671212094-M.jpg?default=false" alt="如何阅读一本书 封面" width="120" loading="lazy" />

不同类型的书需要不同的阅读方法。作者区分基础阅读、检视阅读、分析阅读和主题阅读。最后一种尤其适合这份书单：围绕同一个问题比较黄仁勋、格鲁夫和 Licklider，而不是将每本传记视为独立的成功案例。

## 1. 研究品味与独立思考

### The Art of Doing Science and Engineering

Richard W. Hamming · 1997

《科学与工程的艺术》

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9781732265172-M.jpg?default=false" alt="科学与工程的艺术 封面" width="120" loading="lazy" />

汉明在贝尔实验室的经历使他能够观察优秀研究者的工作方式。本书讨论学习、创造力和问题选择。重点不是成为另一个汉明，而是判断问题的重要性，避免将大量时间投入价值有限的工作。

### Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!

Richard P. Feynman · 1985

《别闹了，费曼先生》

费曼对开锁、绘画和生物学的探索展示了不受专业边界限制的好奇心。独立思考也可能来自游戏、实验和对日常现象的追问。

### A Mind at Play

Jimmy Soni & Rob Goodman · 2017

《香农传》

香农将数学逻辑、电路设计和通信联系起来，奠定信息论的重要基础。他的经历值得用来思考不同领域如何共享相同的抽象结构。

### The Man from the Future

Ananyo Bhattacharya · 2021

《未来之人：冯·诺依曼传》

冯·诺依曼的工作横跨数学、计算机、物理学和博弈论。本书展示深厚的基础知识如何支持跨学科思考，以及抽象如何在不同领域产生价值。

## 2. 技术想象力与先发意识

### The Dream Machine

M. Mitchell Waldrop · 2001

《梦幻机器》

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9781732265110-M.jpg?default=false" alt="梦幻机器 封面" width="120" loading="lazy" />

本书以 Licklider 为核心，讲述交互式计算、个人计算和计算机网络的发展。在批处理占主导的年代，他设想计算机成为人类思考的工具，并通过研究资助、人才组织和实验推动愿景实现。

### The Nvidia Way

Tae Kim · 2024

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9781324086719-M.jpg?default=false" alt="The Nvidia Way 封面" width="120" loading="lazy" />

NVIDIA 的发展也是计算平台的演化史。CUDA 将 GPU 并行计算开放给通用程序员，支持科学计算、工程仿真和后来的深度学习。重点关注既有业务如何支持长期投入，以及如何在不确定的市场中建设开发者生态。

### Steve Jobs

Walter Isaacson · 2011

《史蒂夫·乔布斯传》

乔布斯的经历提供了另一种技术判断力的案例：如何在工程、设计、人文和用户体验之间取舍。技术上可行的产品不一定是好的产品，理解什么应该被舍弃同样重要。

### The Innovators

Walter Isaacson · 2014

《创新者》

从早期计算思想、晶体管到个人电脑和互联网，本书将不同代际的人物与技术联系起来。技术革命往往来自长期积累的知识、工程能力与社会需求的汇合。

## 3. 产业变化与战略判断

### Only the Paranoid Survive

Andrew S. Grove · 1996

《只有偏执狂才能生存》

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780385483827-M.jpg?default=false" alt="只有偏执狂才能生存 封面" width="120" loading="lazy" />

格鲁夫讨论企业如何面对战略转折点。Intel 从存储芯片转向微处理器的经历，是理解如何识别变化、放弃过去的成功路径并重新配置资源的重要案例。

### The Innovator's Dilemma

Clayton M. Christensen · 1997

《创新者的窘境》

为什么管理良好、认真服务客户的企业仍可能错过新技术？Christensen 提供理解颠覆式创新的框架。应结合真实案例检验其解释能力，而不是将所有技术变化都归类为颠覆。

### Chip War

Chris Miller · 2022

《芯片战争》

半导体产业涉及材料、制造工艺、设备、设计软件、资本和国际分工。本书提供理解 NVIDIA、Intel、TSMC 和 ASML 所处产业生态的宏观背景。

### Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital

Carlota Perez · 2002

《技术革命与金融资本》

Perez 研究技术革命、金融资本与经济发展之间的关系。其框架有助于思考投资狂热、先于成熟应用市场的基础设施建设，以及金融泡沫与长期产业价值的关系。

## 4. 创新如何真正发生

### The Idea Factory

Jon Gertner · 2012

《创意工厂》

<img src="https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780143122791-M.jpg?default=false" alt="创意工厂 封面" width="120" loading="lazy" />

贝尔实验室展示了长期投入、人才聚集和跨学科交流如何孕育晶体管、信息论等成果。值得思考的是，哪些组织条件能够支持尚无明确短期商业回报的研究。

### The Soul of a New Machine

Tracy Kidder · 1981

《新机器的灵魂》

本书记录工程师设计新计算机的过程，关注具体工程工作：时间压力、技术争论、团队协作和不完美条件下的设计决策。

### Skunk Works

Ben R. Rich & Leo Janos · 1994

《臭鼬工厂》

洛克希德先进研发团队的故事涉及 U-2、SR-71 和 F-117，展示小型团队如何通过授权、沟通和快速决策完成高度不确定的复杂工程。

### Where Good Ideas Come From

Steven Johnson · 2010

《好点子从哪里来》

Johnson 讨论知识积累、相邻可能性、偶然发现和开放交流。可以与《创意工厂》和《香农传》一起阅读，比较个人创造力与创新环境的关系。

## 5. 更广阔的科学与历史视野

### The Structure of Scientific Revolutions

Thomas S. Kuhn · 1962

《科学革命的结构》

Kuhn 研究科学知识如何在既有范式内发展，以及范式如何发生变化，为理解科学共同体、知识体系和重大理论转变提供视角。

### The Beginning of Infinity

David Deutsch · 2011

《无穷的开始》

本书讨论解释、知识增长和科学进步。值得思考什么是好的解释，以及新知识如何改变看似不可突破的限制。

### The Making of the Atomic Bomb

Richard Rhodes · 1986

《原子弹的诞生》

从基础物理学到曼哈顿计划，本书展示科学发现如何在国家资源、工程组织和历史条件下转化为改变世界的技术，也提醒技术能力增长并不自动意味着社会进步。

### The Wright Brothers

David McCullough · 2015

《莱特兄弟》

莱特兄弟通过实验、测量和持续改进解决动力飞行的关键问题。他们对飞行控制而非单纯发动机功率的重视，是理解问题选择的案例。

## 如何使用这份书单

这份书单不是为了寻找可以复制的成功公式。传记常以最终成就为叙事中心，但更值得关注的是结果尚未确定的时刻。

- 当时有哪些已知信息？哪些趋势已经出现，哪些仍然无法预测？
- 决策者面对哪些替代方案？如果判断错误，需要承担什么代价？
- 同时代的其他人为什么没有做出相同选择？有哪些失败案例？

技术论文、传记和技术史可以互相补充。论文解释内部结构，历史解释出现的条件，人物经历提供观察判断与选择的机会。长期阅读的价值不在于准确预测下一项改变世界的技术，而在于逐渐形成更有依据的判断力。
